Galileus by Artiros

Privacy Policy

Last updated November 22, 2026

  • Galileus collects no data about you.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics, no tracking.
  • Your books, bookmarks and reading history stay on your device.
  • Galileus goes online only to show the online catalog of ZIM books and to download the books you choose.

What Galileus keeps on your device

Galileus is an offline reader for Wikipedia and the other reference libraries published as ZIM files, for iPhone, iPad and Mac, with an Apple Watch app. It keeps the following on your device, in its own storage:

  • The books you download from the catalog, unless you chose another folder for them, such as one on an external drive.
  • Your library: the title, language, size and location of each book you added. Books you open from somewhere else, such as Files, Finder or an external drive, stay where they are; Galileus keeps only a reference to the file so it can open it again.
  • Your bookmarks and the history of the articles you read.
  • Your settings: text size, download folder and the list of downloads in progress.

Articles, their pictures and the search index are read from the book files on your device. Reading and searching work without an internet connection, and Galileus blocks any request a book's page would make to the web. Galileus has no server of its own, and none of this is sent to Artiros or to anyone else. Nothing is synced between your devices: each one keeps its own library.

When Galileus goes online

Galileus connects to the internet for two things only, both started by you:

  • The catalog. When you open the Catalog, Galileus asks the public catalog server for ZIM books for the list of books and their small cover pictures, with the language, category and search words you chose there.
  • Downloads. When you tap Get, the book is downloaded from the download servers of the public catalog server for ZIM books, which may pass the download to a mirror run by another organization, such as a university.

These requests contain only what any web request does. They carry no account, no identifier and nothing from your library, bookmarks or history. Like any web request, they reach those servers from your device's internet address, and the operators of the catalog and of the mirrors handle them under their own policies. They are independent of Artiros, which receives nothing from these requests.

Web links in an article open in your default web browser, outside Galileus. What happens there is governed by your browser and by the website you visit.

Apple Watch

When you choose Send to Apple Watch on your iPhone, the book file is copied from your iPhone to your paired Apple Watch by the system, over Bluetooth or Wi-Fi. The watch keeps its own copy and reads it offline. The file goes from one of your devices to the other; Artiros does not receive it.

What Galileus does not do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, crash reporting or advertising tools, and no third-party code.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no sharing or selling of data.
  • No access to your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or health data. Galileus asks for no permissions; it opens only the files you choose.

Backups and diagnostics from Apple

Books saved in Galileus's own folder are kept out of your device's backups, because they are large and can be downloaded again. Your library list, bookmarks, history and settings can be included in the backups your device makes for you, such as iCloud Backup, a backup on your computer or Time Machine. Those backups belong to you and are managed by Apple's services, not by Artiros.

If you turned on sharing with app developers in your device's privacy settings, Apple may share crash reports and usage statistics with developers. That comes from Apple under your settings; Galileus itself contains no code that collects them.

Delete your data

  • History: open History and choose Clear History.
  • Bookmarks: in Bookmarks, swipe a bookmark to delete it, or touch and hold it (on a Mac, Control-click it) and choose Remove Bookmark.
  • Books: in My Books, touch and hold a book (on a Mac, Control-click it) and choose Remove from Library, which keeps the file, or Delete File, which deletes a book kept in Galileus's download folder (on a Mac, the file goes to the Trash). Removing a book also removes it from your history.
  • To delete everything on iPhone or iPad, delete the app: touch and hold the Galileus icon, tap Remove App, then Delete App. Its data and the books in its own folder are deleted with it. Deleting Galileus from Apple Watch deletes the books sent to the watch.
  • On a Mac, move Galileus from Applications to the Trash. Its data folder, the Galileus folder in ~/Library/Containers, can then be moved to the Trash too.
  • Books you keep in another folder or on an external drive stay there until you delete them yourself. Copies in your own backups are under your control.

Children

Galileus collects no personal information from anyone, so it collects none from children. The books in the catalog are general reference works written for a general audience, such as encyclopedias and dictionaries; parents may want to choose the books a child downloads.

Changes to this policy

If Galileus's handling of data ever changes, this page will be updated before the new version of the app is released, with a new date at the top. The privacy details on the App Store will be updated at the same time.
